Studio Who designs it

Seoyoon HanHan Seoyun

Principal · Registered Architect (KIRA) · founder of Ongyeol Architects

What we do now. She takes the first site visit and the concept design on every project that comes into the studio. Through technical design she keeps hold of the principal sections and the window, stair, and handrail details to the end, and on supervised sites she visits every two weeks to check drawings against what has actually been built.

How we work. We draw the section before the plan. Once you know how far light reaches at standing height, and where the ceiling drops and where it opens, the plan usually follows. Models are always made at both 1:100 and 1:20, and client meetings run on models and site photographs rather than drawings.

The road so far. Seven years from 2004 in an office working at the scale of civic projects, where I watched large buildings get better or worse on a single small decision, more than once. Ongyeol opened in 2011 and has completed 34 projects since — houses, small public buildings, and the renewal of older ones.

What we hold to. I think an architect makes judgements rather than forms. Most of the work is deciding where not to put a window and which material not to use — and being able to explain the reasoning to the client, all the way through.

Philosophy The judgement behind the design

Four principles.
The same sequence of checks on every project.

Not fine words but the tests that actually change a drawing. Under each principle: what we hold to, what we therefore do, and the building where it shows.

  1. 01Site first

    Let the site speak first

    "When a building does not argue with what the site has already decided, it looks as though it has been there a long time."

    The slope, a neighbour's shadow, the path of the wind, one old tree — these are not constraints on the design, they are where it starts. A form the site will not allow does not get built, however well it draws.

    So we visit the site at least twice before signing, at different hours. The shadow line at 9 AM in winter and the wind direction at 4 PM in summer are measured and drawn onto a single site analysis sheet — and that one sheet is the basis for every decision after it.

    Evidence — Gyeopjip, Nuha-dong, Seochon (2023)

    The first floor was dropped 60cm so as not to rise above the eaves of the hanok to the north. Winter noon light now reaches 3m further into the south courtyard.

  2. 02Light plan

    Begin the plan from the direction of light

    "Before a window is something to look out of, it decides which hour of the day comes into the room."

    What a room is comes from the angle of the light and how long it stays, not the floor area. Morning light in the bedroom, even north light in the study, afternoon light in the kitchen — those are our defaults, and when we depart from them we write down why.

    Every project gets daylight studies on three dates — winter solstice, summer solstice, equinox — run in a 1:100 model and in software. When the two disagree, we trust the model.

    Evidence — Sacheon Stacks, Gangneung (2024)

    So that direct sun reaches the east reading room only between 7 and 10 AM and nothing but reflected light after, the shelf depth (420mm) and window height (2,700mm) were settled together.

  3. 03Material aging

    Choose materials by how they age

    "We choose materials imagining the wall ten years on, not the completion photograph."

    We prefer materials that improve under rain and handling to those that are most beautiful when new. Maintenance costs and replacement cycles are discussed with the client in numbers, early. If the roof needs replacing once in twenty years, that cost goes into the first budget sheet.

    Candidate cladding is weathered on the studio roof for at least one season. Before construction, a full-size mock-up (1,200×1,800mm) is stood up on site for the client to see before anything is confirmed.

    Evidence — Slow Kiln, Yangpyeong (2022)

    The cladding is unpainted cedar. Three years on, the south face has gone silver-grey and the north a deep brown. That was the intention from the start.

  4. 04Detail on site

    The drawing is a promise; the last decision is made on site

    "A 1:20 detail is a letter to the builder. We have to know how it will be made before they do."

    Quality is settled at the joint, not in the concept. How a handrail meets the wall, the 3mm where a window frame meets the finish — that is what a room feels like.

    Stairs, windows, handrails, and eaves are always drawn at 1:5 or larger, and the principal personally checks them against the builder's shop drawings. Site visits every two weeks are recorded with photographs and measurements, and any decision changed on site reaches the client that day.

    Evidence — House of Type, Euljiro (2021)

    The detail where the existing steel column meets the new timber stair was revised three times on site. The final version was simpler than the drawing, and that record went to the client with the completion set.

On a narrow plot in a dense hanok district, the first condition was not to rise above the neighbour's eaves to the north. Process How it runs

From the first meeting
through to the one-year review

For a house, design to completion usually runs 14–18 months. For each stage we have separated what the client decides from what we verify. Tap a stage name for the detail.

Before what to build, we listen to why this building is needed now.

  1. Ninety minutes at the first meeting, listening and taking notes: who lives there, how a day moves through the house, what might change in ten years.
  2. Together we draw up a first allocation, splitting the total across design, construction, permits, furniture, and contingency.
  3. The gap between the move-in date you want and what is actually possible, in numbers.
  4. Both sides decide whether we are the right practice. If it stops here, there is no fee.

Deliverables

A brief, a budget allocation, an outline programme

What the client decides

Whether to proceed, and the design contract. The first site visit is scheduled after signing

What the ground has already decided, set down on a single sheet.

  1. At least two visits at different hours — morning and late afternoon — measuring shadow, wind, noise, and sightlines.
  2. Discrepancies between the survey and the cadastral map, the boundaries with neighbouring plots, and whether existing retaining walls and trees can stay.
  3. Zoning, site coverage, floor area ratio, daylight setbacks, and road conditions are set out in a planning review, with the maximum permissible envelope calculated.
  4. Ground investigation and the terms for water, drainage, and power are confirmed early, to take variables out of construction.

Deliverables

One site analysis sheet, planning review, seasonal daylight diagrams

What the client decides

What to keep — trees, walls, parts of the existing building — and what to let go

Starting from section and light, two schemes, then one.

  1. Two schemes presented in the order site, section, plan, each with a 1:100 model, and compared against the same set of criteria.
  2. Daylight studies at the winter solstice, summer solstice, and equinox, run in both model and software, to verify light in the principal rooms.
  3. Structural options (timber, reinforced concrete, steel) and candidate cladding, set out with outline costs.
  4. With a scheme chosen, we check again that the outline cost still sits inside the budget allocation.

Deliverables

Concept set (site plan, plans, sections, elevations), 1:100 model, outline cost

What the client decides

One of the two schemes. The direction for structure and cladding

A 1:20 detail is a letter to the builder. We draw the joint first.

  1. Stairs, windows, handrails, and eaves are always drawn at 1:5 or larger and reviewed by the principal.
  2. Structural, mechanical, and electrical drawings checked floor by floor against the architectural set for clashes.
  3. Cladding is confirmed only after a full-size mock-up (1,200×1,800mm) has been stood up on site or at the studio.
  4. Alongside the permit submission we prepare the tender set and put together a comparison of the bids.

Deliverables

Full technical set (architectural, structural, mechanical, electrical), specification, tender comparison

What the client decides

Final materials and finishes; choosing the builder

On site we are not checking whether it matches the drawing, but whether it can be better than the drawing.

  1. Site visits every two weeks, with the principal present at four moments without exception: frame, waterproofing, window installation, and the start of finishes.
  2. The builder's shop drawings are checked against our details, and anything changed on site is recorded with photographs and dimensions and shared with the client.
  3. A site report of no more than two pages reaches you within 48 hours of each visit.
  4. One year after completion we return, and record how the materials have changed and what has proved awkward in use.

Deliverables

Site reports every two weeks, the completion set, and the one-year review record

What the client decides

Approving changes made on site; scheduling the completion inspection
