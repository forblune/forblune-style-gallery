Knee, shoulder, and spine pain — we find the cause and treat only as much as is needed.

Three orthopaedic specialists practise here. We establish the cause through examination and imaging first, then explain which of medication, injection, manual therapy, or rehabilitation is called for — and decide it with you.

Specialties

What we look for in each area, and how we test and treat it. If you are not sure which it falls under, say so at reception and we will point you to the right doctor.

Area · specialtyWhat we commonly seeHow we test and treatDoctor

KneeFrom osteoarthritis to ligament injury

What we commonly see

Pain on stairs, swelling, clicking, and locking. Osteoarthritis, meniscal tears, ACL injuries, chondromalacia patellae.

Tests and treatment

We check joint space on X-ray and look at cartilage and ligaments on ultrasound if needed. Treatment starts with medication and physical therapy, moving to viscosupplementation and exercise therapy depending on the joint. For ligament injuries we set how long a brace should be worn.

DoctorDr Dohyun KimMon · Tue · Thu · Fri · Sat

ShoulderRotator cuff · frozen shoulder · calcific tendinitis

What we commonly see

Pain lifting the arm or reaching behind, and shoulder pain that worsens at night. Rotator cuff tears, frozen shoulder, calcific tendinitis, impingement.

Tests and treatment

Ultrasound shows the extent of a tear and the size of any calcification, right there in the room. Range-of-motion work and ultrasound-guided injection come first, with shockwave for calcific tendinitis. For a large tear we discuss surgery at a partner hospital together.

DoctorDr Seoyoon LeeMon · Wed · Thu · Fri · Sat

Spine (neck and back)Disc · stenosis · acute low back pain

What we commonly see

Numbness running into a leg or arm, back pain that worsens the longer you walk, a neck that stiffens overnight. Disc herniation, spinal stenosis, acute low back pain, forward head posture.

Tests and treatment

We check for nerve signs such as numbness and weakness first, then X-ray, and refer for an MRI the same day if needed. Medication, physical therapy, and manual therapy come first; where pain is severe we perform a C-arm guided nerve block.

DoctorDr Junyoung ParkMon · Tue · Wed · Fri · Sat

Sports injuriesAnkle · sole · elbow · thigh

What we commonly see

A joint that swells or gives way after a sporting injury, and heel pain on the first step of the morning. Ankle sprains, Achilles tendinitis, plantar fasciitis, tennis and golfer's elbow, hamstring tears.

Tests and treatment

Ultrasound shows how far a ligament or tendon is damaged, with an X-ray added if a fracture is suspected. We set how many weeks to brace and when to return to sport, and plan shockwave therapy for chronic tendinitis.

DoctorDr Dohyun KimMon · Tue · Thu · Fri · Sat

Manual therapyOne-to-one with a physiotherapist, on prescription

For

Chronic neck and back pain, shoulders and knees that have lost range, pain that keeps returning from postural imbalance. Prescribed only when the doctor judges it necessary after examination.

How it works

A physiotherapist works on joint mobility and muscle tension in 30- or 50-minute sessions, recording the plan and your response each time to shape the next one. Not covered by insurance: KRW 70,000 for 30 minutes, KRW 100,000 for 50.

Doctor6 physiotherapistsFour manual therapy rooms on the fourth floor · Costs

Rehabilitation and exercise therapyPost-surgical · chronic pain · posture

For

Recovering joint angles after surgery, strength lost to chronic pain, and the postural problems behind repeated injury.

How it works

Pain score and joint angle are measured every session and compared with the last. Across 12 physical therapy beds we combine shockwave with strength and balance work as prescribed, and send you home with illustrated exercises.

From arrival to rehabilitation, in five steps

What to bring and how it runs, so a first visit holds no surprises. The times are averages and vary with what needs testing.

  1. 1

    Registration

    About 5 min

    Show ID at the third-floor reception desk and fill in a symptom questionnaire.

    • Photo ID (required at a first visit), plus any imaging discs and reports from elsewhere
    • Booked patients can check in at the kiosk
  2. 2

    Consultation

    About 10–15 min

    The doctor takes your history and examines you — joint angles, tenderness, strength.

    • We ask when it started, how it happened, and what you are taking
    • We choose only the tests that are needed and say why — sometimes a prescription is the whole of it
  3. 3

    Tests

    About 10–20 min

    X-rays are ready the moment they are taken; ultrasound is performed by the doctor in the consulting room, explained on screen as it happens.

    • X-ray · musculoskeletal ultrasound · bone density
    • Referred to a partner radiology clinic if an MRI is needed
    • Results explained the same day in the consulting room
  4. 4

    Treatment

    About 20–50 min

    We set out which of medication, injection, physical therapy, shockwave, or manual therapy is needed — with the cost — before starting.

    • The cost of anything not covered is stated before it is done
    • Injections are placed under ultrasound guidance
    • Physical therapy happens the same day on the fourth floor
    • Tell us at this point if you plan to claim on indemnity insurance
  5. 5

    Rehabilitation and follow-up

    planned over 2–8 weeks

    We set how often to come back and what to do at home, and record pain scores and joint angles so progress can be compared.

    • You go home with an illustrated exercise sheet
    • If there is no improvement after two sessions, we rebuild the plan

Worth knowing beforehand

Manual therapy is not covered by national insurance, and what your indemnity policy pays back depends on its terms — limits and session counts differ by policy generation. We issue the itemised bill, receipt, and a treatment certificate if needed on the same day so you can claim. Whether it is reimbursed is the insurer's decision, so the clinic cannot promise that it will be.

We do not have an MRI scanner. If one is needed, we send a referral to Mapo Radiology Clinic seven minutes away and book you in for the same or the next day. Your own doctor here then goes through the images and the report with you.

Ultrasound guidance keeps unnecessary pain down, though the site can ache for a day or two. We keep steroid injections to no more than three or four a year in the same place, and check beforehand with anyone diabetic, as blood sugar can rise briefly. Whatever the injection, we explain the purpose, the expected effect, and the risks, and take your consent first.
